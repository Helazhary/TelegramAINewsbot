## 1. Banned Nvidia Blackwell racks turn up in a Shanghai data center (Bloomberg, Nov 27)

**Bloomberg** reviewed loan filings and found that two Chinese companies installed **Nvidia Blackwell** hardware at a Shanghai site linked to **Alice Huang**. **Shanghai Shuoyao** pledged a **GB300 NVL72 rack** as collateral for a **96M yuan (~$14M) loan** from Shanghai Innovation Bank. **Shanghai Kezhan** listed a **GB200 server** at the same site in documents for a 90M yuan loan from a finance-leasing firm owned by a local government agency. Kezhan also disclosed **32 H200 servers** there. Shuoyao says it provides services to **Megaspeed**, the Nvidia customer the US blacklisted on Nov 24 over chip-diversion risk. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and Bloomberg says it has **no evidence Megaspeed directly diverted** the equipment. **How the hardware got into China is unconfirmed.**

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Expect **more enforcement**: stricter KYC from GPU clouds, slower server deliveries in Asia, and more paperwork for anyone reselling or hosting high-end compute. If you depend on GPU capacity in Asia, plan for disruption.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **chip-provenance and diversion-risk monitor**. Ingest public corporate, loan-collateral and customs filings (including Chinese-language registries), entity lists and BIS updates. An LLM extracts hardware mentions such as "GB300 NVL72" and links entities into a graph, so exporters, OEMs and GPU clouds get flagged when a customer is linked to a suspicious site. Sell it as compliance tooling to Dell/Supermicro-tier resellers and neoclouds.

## 2. OpenAI fixes a GPT-6.2 metering bug, so ChatGPT limits now run out up to 40% sooner (Nov 26–27)

OpenAI's **Thibault Sottiaux** said a launch-day mistake had been **undercounting usage** of **GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol**. With the fix, the same workload uses **Astra limits ~40% faster** and **Sol limits ~25% faster** than last week. OpenAI **won't charge retroactively**. Paid subscribers (Go, Plus, Pro, Business) get **one banked limit reset**, which they can redeem under Usage settings on web or desktop (not yet on mobile). Business users reportedly get **free Fast Mode through the end of 2026**. OpenAI says its **pricing pages haven't changed**. One outlet reports that credit-paid overage costs 3× the standard rate through February, but that is **single-source**.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** If you budgeted agent or Codex workflows on last week's limits, **re-measure now**. Your effective cost per task just went up, and with ChatGPT and Codex moving to one shared meter in December, burn rates will be harder to predict.

Charge per seat.

## 3. Microsoft: Chinese and North Korean state hackers now run the ARTEX AI pentest agent (report ~Nov 25, covered Nov 27)

**Microsoft Threat Intelligence** says **ARTEX**, an open-source, **Go-based multi-agent AI penetration-testing framework** released in July 2026, is now in the toolkits of **Salt Typhoon, Crystal Typhoon, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Storm-0940** and **Storm-3358**. Operators set the scope, and the agents plan steps, run standard tools, build a graph of discovered assets and report back. Microsoft stresses that ARTEX uses **no new techniques**; its effect is **speed and less human involvement**. One Storm-0940 Microsoft 365 password spray logged more than **151,000 failed sign-ins**, peaking at **2,900 per minute**, and some campaigns reached **privileged access in under three hours**. Indicators include the user agents **`artex-enrich`** and **`norma`**, which operators change. Chinese security firm **ThreatBook** claims ownership and sells an "ARTEX Pro"; Microsoft does **not** link ThreatBook to the attacks. Claims of an FBI alert and 130+ variants are **single-source and unverified**.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Attackers now move at **agent speed**, so a weekly review of sign-in logs is too slow. Enforce phishing-resistant MFA, rate-limit auth endpoints and alert on spray patterns, including for small SaaS products.
- **Business/project idea:** Build an **"agentic attacker" detection pack**: rules for Sentinel, Splunk and Elastic plus a lightweight SaaS that fingerprints machine-speed attack sequences (enumeration → spray → lateral movement within minutes) rather than static user agents. Pair it with a **defensive red-team-as-a-service** that runs a sanctioned agent against a customer's own tenant to show their time-to-compromise.

## 4. Anthropic's Claude Duet work agent expands to India, Indonesia, Brazil and Japan (Nov 25–26)

**Claude Duet**, Anthropic's **proactive work-agent app**, launched as a US-only research preview in October. It is now opening via **waitlist** in **India, Indonesia, Brazil and Japan**, with Japan going live on Nov 26. Duet watches your **email, calendar and connected tools**, sends a **morning brief**, drafts replies and next steps, takes on multi-step jobs like scheduling or building dashboards, and **asks for approval on critical actions**. It carries over your **Claude memory, skills and 6,000+ connectors**. During the preview it costs **nothing extra** for **Pro, Max and Ultra** subscribers and draws on the normal usage limits. Team plans roll out through admins. There is **no EU or UK date** yet.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Proactive agents now reach big, mobile-first markets like India, Indonesia and Brazil. If your SaaS is **reachable as a connector**, Duet can bring it into users' daily workflow; if it isn't, it gets left out.

Package each with Claude **skills** (for example "reconcile today's Pix receipts"), list them in the connector directory, and charge SMBs a subscription.

## 5. Reuters: Z.ai's GLM-5.5 slips past November, possibly to 2027, as US compute curbs bite (Nov 26)

Citing two anonymous sources, **Reuters** reports that **Z.ai (Zhipu)** missed its November target for its flagship **GLM-5.5** (reported at ~3T parameters). The launch could slip to **Q1 2027 or later**, with one source saying around mid-2027. The reasons given are **tighter anti-distillation safeguards** at US labs and the **Nov 19 BIS rule** requiring overseas data centers to get a **license to serve Chinese AI firms** with large amounts of compute. The same report says **Moonshot's Kimi K4** could slip to around mid-2027 if its overseas compute is cut off. Z.ai didn't comment to Reuters. One outlet says a Z.ai executive later **denied** the delay, but that denial is **unverified**.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** If you rely on cheap **Chinese open-weight models** (GLM, Kimi) for cost-sensitive workloads, expect **longer gaps between major releases**. Don't plan around a GLM-5.5 date, and keep a fallback open-weight option such as Western or Japanese models.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **model-roadmap risk tracker** for teams that self-host open weights. It tracks the release cadence, licences and regulatory exposure (BIS rules, entity lists) of each open-model family, and when a dependency looks at risk it auto-generates a migration eval: your prompts run on candidate replacement models, scored by an LLM judge.
