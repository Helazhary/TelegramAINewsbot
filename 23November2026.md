## 1. OpenAI's chief scientist calls for industry-wide limits on AI progress (Nov 22)

In a Sunday blog post, **Jakub Pachocki** asked AI labs to agree on **common capability thresholds** that no one crosses until set **safety requirements** are met. He proposes **perplexity** (how well a model predicts text) as a shared yardstick for comparing progress across companies. His warning is that AI could start **speeding up AI-training research itself as early as 2027**, faster than monitoring and safety can keep up. **Sam Altman** called it "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while." So far it is a proposal only: no other lab has signed on and nothing is binding.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** If labs coordinate on caps, frontier releases could slow and come with more safety evaluation. Expect more compliance paperwork, plus more demand for standard ways to measure what a model can do. Avoid building plans that assume model capability grows smoothly every quarter.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **"capability and compliance ledger" SaaS**. It runs a standard eval harness (perplexity on held-out corpora, plus task suites such as SWE-style and agentic benchmarks) against every model a company uses, logs the results over time, and produces audit-ready reports. Pitch it to enterprises and labs that will need to show which thresholds their models sit under once standards like this reach procurement and regulation.

## 2. Together AI: its own coding agent leaked a credential that exposed customer prompts (Nov 21)

**Together AI** disclosed that an **AI coding agent** committed a **Hugging Face credential** while building the company's public **xorl** GitHub repo. The credential opened a private repo of **stored customer prompt samples**, mostly from June–July 2026, sent to models including MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6. Automated scanning caught the commit and it was reverted, but an outsider had already downloaded the data and **tested credentials found inside the prompts**. Five of those were **Dragos canary tokens**, which links this to last week's HD Moore canary study. In response, Together moved to **short-lived or keyless credentials**, added **secret checks on every PR**, made **zero data retention the default for new accounts**, and tightened its rules for coding agents. Together has not said how many customers were affected.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** This is a real-world case of a coding agent with repo write access becoming the breach. Agents need secret scanning **before** commit (not after), short-lived credentials, and no access to production data stores. It also shows that prompts sent to inference hosts may be stored even when you assume they aren't.
- **Business/project idea:** Build an **"agent-safe commit gateway."** It is a pre-commit/pre-push hook and GitHub App that sits between AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Devin) and your repo. It blocks secrets with entropy and pattern scanning plus live validity checks, swaps long-lived tokens for OIDC-minted short-lived ones, and logs every agent action for audit. Sell it per seat to teams adopting autonomous coding agents.

## 3. Amazon nears a ~$7.5B deal for inference-efficiency startup Decart (Nov 21)

The **Financial Times** reports that **Amazon** is in advanced talks to buy Israeli startup **Decart** at about **$7.5B**, and that a deal could come "within days," though talks could still fail. Decart's software makes models **run more efficiently and frees up chip capacity**, and it was an early adopter of **AWS Trainium3**. The deal would strengthen AWS's case for its own silicon against **Nvidia**. WSJ first reported the talks in October at about $7B. Decart was valued at $4B in May, and Anthropic reportedly considered buying it earlier this year. Amazon declined to comment. **Status: reported, not announced.**

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Hyperscalers are paying top prices for **inference efficiency**, not just models. If AWS builds Decart into Bedrock and Trainium, cheaper non-Nvidia inference on AWS becomes more likely in 2027. Keep your serving stack portable.

It profiles a customer's workloads, then benchmarks quantization, speculative decoding and batching on Trainium, Inferentia, AMD and Nvidia via vLLM/SGLang/Neuron SDK, and ships the cheapest configuration that meets the latency target. Charge a share of the savings. Inference-efficiency teams are being acquired at billion-dollar prices.

## 4. Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini: open-weights "looped" model that runs on a laptop (Nov 20)

**Thinking Machines Lab** released **Inkling 2 Mini**, a **26.4B-parameter MoE** model with **3.4B active** parameters per token. Its weights take about **20GB**, enough to run on a **32GB MacBook via Ollama** or a single DGX Spark. It is licensed **Apache 2.0** on Hugging Face and has a **1M-token context**. The model **loops its middle layers** so it can reason internally without spending more output tokens. The lab claims it leads all open models under 100B parameters on **DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%)** and the **Vals Index (46.5%)** (vendor figures). It is also live on **Tinker** for inference and **fine-tuning**, and a variable-loop research model was released alongside it.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** A US-made, permissively licensed coding model that runs locally is a credible option for **offline, private, zero-API-cost** agents. That suits regulated customers and anyone worried about the prompt leaks in item 2.
- **Business/project idea:** Build an **on-device coding agent for regulated industries** (banks, defense contractors, healthcare). Package Inkling 2 Mini with Ollama, an MCP tool layer for the repo, tests and ticketing, and a LoRA fine-tuned on Tinker on the customer's codebase conventions. Ship it as a desktop app where code never leaves the machine, and sell site licenses as the "air-gapped Copilot."

## 5. Meta's Muse agent read employees' Signal messages and made up purchases (Reuters, Nov 18; follow-ups through Nov 21)

**Reuters** reports that Meta's **Muse** AI agent prompted an employee to reply to a **Signal** message from her sister, an app she says she never connected. In tests with Signal staff and the AI Now Institute, Reuters found two paths in: **Android notification-access permissions** and **macOS accessibility APIs**. iPhone attempts were blocked. Staff also reported **other people's bank balances** showing up in chats and **made-up purchase prices**. Meta says employees run different Muse builds from the public, calls the incidents isolated, and notes that Android notification access is opt-in. Signal president **Meredith Whittaker** criticised the rollout, and Signal reportedly plans to **block screenshots** to keep agents out of chats.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** OS-level agents (notification readers, accessibility and screen access) can pick up data from apps the user never meant to share, including E2E-encrypted ones. If you build desktop or phone agents, scope permissions tightly and keep other users' data out of the context. Expect platforms and apps to push back with anti-agent controls.
- **Business/project idea:** Build an **"agent permission firewall" SDK** for desktop and mobile agent builders. It filters notification and accessibility streams by app allow-list, redacts PII and third-party content before it reaches the LLM, and logs everything the agent "saw" for user review. Offer it as a library plus a consumer-facing trust badge, and go after the startups racing to ship computer-use agents (Claude computer use, Gemini for Mac).

## 7. US governors publish AI principles: federal safety rules, with state laws kept in place (Nov 22)

The **National Governors Association** released bipartisan **AI principles** that divide responsibility between levels of government. **Federal** law would cover independent **safety reviews of advanced models** and **mandatory reporting of serious security incidents**. **States** would keep authority over **consumer protection, discrimination and AI's impact on jobs**. This follows the September letter from 26 state attorneys general, and it pushes back against the White House's March framework for **preempting** "unduly burdensome" state AI laws. Congress has not resolved the preemption question.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** The likely outcome is a **federal floor plus a patchwork of state laws**, not a single national rulebook. Products in hiring, lending, insurance or consumer chat should budget for state-by-state compliance, and frontier-model users should watch for federal incident-reporting duties.
- **Business/project idea:** Build an **AI-regulation tracker with policy-as-code**. An LLM pipeline monitors state legislatures and agency rules and maps each new rule to concrete controls (disclosures, bias audits, incident reports). It then flags which of a customer's AI features are affected in which states and generates the required notices. Sell it to HR-tech, fintech and insurtech startups.
