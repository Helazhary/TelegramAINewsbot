The biggest story of the past 24 hours is Nvidia's extraordinary **$750+ billion weekend of AI infrastructure dealmaking** — a **$250 billion financing backstop** to help OpenAI lease a 10-gigawatt Ohio data-center campus, layered on top of a **$500 billion HBM4/AI-factory pact** with SK Hynix and SK Telecom — which sent Nvidia's own stock down 4% on renewed "circular financing" fears just as Amazon and Microsoft brace investors for their own quarterly AI-spending numbers.

## 1. Nvidia's $750B AI dealmaking weekend: $250B OpenAI Ohio backstop + $500B SK Hynix pact

Nvidia is in talks to provide a roughly **$250 billion financing guarantee** so OpenAI can lease a **10-gigawatt data-center campus** that SoftBank subsidiary SB Energy is building on a former uranium-enrichment site in Piketon, Ohio (full project cost, including chips, could exceed $500B); a separate discussion could see Nvidia back another $350B in chip purchases. Days earlier, Nvidia and **SK Group** announced Letters of Intent for a **$500B+ partnership**: SK Hynix will supply roughly 70% of the first HBM4 memory orders for Nvidia's next-gen **Vera Rubin** platform, and SK Telecom will build a 2-gigawatt AI-factory campus running on it, with phase one live in 2027. The combined weekend of commitments topped $750B and pulled Nvidia shares down 4% on Monday as investors weighed "circular financing" risk — since OpenAI has no investment-grade credit rating of its own, Nvidia effectively vouches for demand for its own chips.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Compute supply and pricing for the next several years is being locked up in these mega-deals — expect continued GPU/HBM scarcity for smaller buyers, but also a coming wave of cheaper capacity once these campuses (2027–2028) come online.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **compute-capacity forecasting/brokerage tool** for startups — tracking announced data-center timelines (Ohio, SK Telecom Korea, etc.) against GPU spot pricing to help teams time reserved-instance purchases or arbitrage between cloud providers as new capacity lands.

## 2. Claude's "share" links exposed private chats in Google Search results

A Reddit post this past weekend revealed that hundreds of **Claude AI shared-chat and Artifact links** were publicly discoverable via Google (searches like `site:claude.ai/share`), exposing conversations containing **legal advice, engineering work, API keys, resumes, and even a real patient's medical/clinical-trial records with names attached**. The exposure happened because once a user set a chat to "anyone with a link," the resulting page lacked a `noindex` tag, so if that link was ever posted anywhere public (a forum, a social post), search engines could crawl and index it — Google says it had indexed just under 600 such pages before they dropped from results. Anthropic says the links themselves aren't guessable, and this is a repeat of a similar incident last year.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** If your product or workflow uses Claude's (or any LLM's) "share" feature to hand off conversations, treat every shared link as potentially public forever — audit for `noindex`/robots rules before you rely on "share" links for anything containing customer or regulated data.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **"shared-link privacy scanner"** browser extension or SaaS that audits an org's AI-tool share links (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) for public indexing exposure and flags PII/secrets before they leak — a natural add-on for compliance/security teams already worried about shadow AI usage.

## 3. Amazon and Microsoft set to spend ~$400B combined on AI infrastructure this year, investors growing wary

Ahead of Microsoft's fiscal Q4 earnings (July 29) and Amazon's (July 30), reporting shows the two companies are each on pace to spend **roughly $200 billion this year** on AI data centers — a combined **~$400 billion** — as they race not to fall behind Google in the cloud/AI buildout. Microsoft's AI revenue has already topped a **$37B annualized run rate**, and its commercial backlog (RPO) hit **$627B**, up 99% year-over-year. But after Alphabet raised its own capex forecast by up to $15B, investors are increasingly nervous about debt-fueled capex outpacing near-term returns across Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Hyperscaler capex at this scale is what underwrites falling-but-volatile API/inference prices — but investor "patience" running thin means a correction (pricing hikes, slower feature rollouts, or a pullback in free-tier generosity) is a real near-term risk to plan around.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **multi-cloud cost-hedging layer** for AI-heavy products — one that abstracts inference across AWS Bedrock, Azure OpenAI, and GCP Vertex so a customer's app can fail over or reprice automatically if any one provider raises rates or throttles capacity.

## 4. Nvidia's "open weights" policy letter doubles to 50+ signatories — Anthropic and Amazon stay out

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** This is a real fork in AI policy — if open-weight models get regulatory backing while any future restrictions target closed labs differently (or vice versa), it will directly shape which models you can legally self-host, fine-tune, or export.
- **Business/project idea:** Launch a **regulatory-tracking dashboard for AI builders** that monitors open-vs-closed-weight policy signals (this letter, EU AI Act updates, potential Chinese-model bans) so teams choosing between self-hosted open models and closed APIs can plan for compliance risk before committing architecture.

## 5. Fields Medalist Jacob Tsimerman pivots to AI safety, joins OpenAI

Mathematician **Jacob Tsimerman**, awarded the 2026 **Fields Medal** (math's top prize) at the International Congress of Mathematicians on July 23 for reshaping o-minimality theory and proving the André–Oort conjecture, announced at the award ceremony that he is "pivoting toward AI safety" and will join **OpenAI's** safety division in August. Tsimerman has publicly warned that AI could soon surpass human mathematicians and poses serious risk, framing his move as trying to find mathematical rigor for AI-safety guarantees from inside a frontier lab; OpenAI's Mark Chen, Greg Brockman, and others publicly welcomed him.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** A world-class pure mathematician moving into applied AI safety signals that formal/provable safety guarantees (not just empirical red-teaming) are becoming a serious, funded research direction at frontier labs — worth watching if you're building anything in AI verification, evals, or interpretability tooling.
- **Business/project idea:** Build **formal-verification tooling for AI agent behavior** (e.g., provable constraints on tool-calling scope or output bounds) — a niche but increasingly investable category as labs recruit theoretical talent into safety, creating early customers among labs and enterprises deploying agents with real-world permissions.

## 6. Perplexity ships pplx, a terminal CLI for its Search API built for coding agents

Perplexity released **pplx**, a single-binary command-line client that exposes its Search API directly in the terminal, returning grounded web-search results and cleaned page text as JSON — designed to be called equally by humans and by coding agents. It supports modes for standard web search, deep research, code-specific search (finding libraries/syntax/debugging help), and academic search, with results piping cleanly into `jq` or straight into an agent's tool-call loop. Pricing is $5 per 1,000 requests, capped at 50 QPS on every tier.

- **Why this matters for you (developer/entrepreneur):** Gives coding agents (Claude Code, Copilot, custom agent stacks) a cheap, structured, low-friction way to ground themselves in live web data instead of relying on a model's built-in browsing — useful wherever you're building or extending an agent that needs current information.
- **Business/project idea:** Build a **research-augmented coding agent** or IDE plugin that wires `pplx code`/`pplx research` into an existing agent loop (e.g., a Claude Code MCP tool or a Copilot extension) so it auto-fetches current library docs, CVEs, or API changes mid-task instead of hallucinating from stale training data.
